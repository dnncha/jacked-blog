19:T15fe,<p>You finish your last set. What's your next weight?</p>
<p>If you do not know, you are doing more work than necessary to plan the next session.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-auto-progression">What Is Auto-Progression?</h2>
<p>Auto-progression means the app turns what you just logged into a suggested next load, rep target, or repeat decision.</p>
<p>Hit your rep target? Weight goes up next session.
Didn't hit it? Same weight or deload.</p>
<p>Less spreadsheet work. Less guesswork. No promise that plateaus disappear.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why It Matters</h2>
<p><strong>Manual tracking can fail</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>You forget what you lifted last time</li>
<li>You don't calculate the increase</li>
<li>You lose the thread between sessions</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>A useful progression system makes these easier to see:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Whether the target was met</li>
<li>Whether to add load, repeat the target, or reduce the demand</li>
<li>Whether fatigue or recovery evidence suggests caution</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-research">The Research</h2>
<p>The useful principle is simpler than a headline percentage: consistent progression is easier to execute when the target is visible and connected to the last comparable session. An app can reduce the friction of applying that principle, but it cannot promise a result.</p>
<h2 id="a-safer-progression-rule">A Safer Progression Rule</h2>
<p>Use the same exercise, setup, and target range long enough to make comparisons meaningful. Then use a simple rule:</p>
<ul>
<li>If every work set reaches the top of the rep range with the planned effort, add the smallest useful load increase.</li>
<li>If performance lands inside the range but effort is high, repeat the load and try to add a rep next time.</li>
<li>If you miss the bottom of the range, repeat the load or reduce it slightly; do not force an increase because the calendar says so.</li>
<li>If several exercises regress together, look at sleep, food, stress, illness, and accumulated fatigue before changing the whole program.</li>
</ul>
<p>The right increase is the smallest change that preserves technique and leaves the next session recoverable. That is usually more useful than chasing a fixed percentage.</p>
<h2 id="what-an-app-cannot-decide">What an App Cannot Decide</h2>
<p>An algorithm sees the data you record. It does not see pain, poor technique, a change in equipment, or a stressful week unless you record that context. A suggested load is therefore a starting point, not an instruction. Skip or modify a recommendation when it conflicts with symptoms, safety, or the plan from a qualified coach or clinician.</p>

<p>Surpass is designed to make the next decision visible:</p>
<ol>
<li>Tracks every set, rep, and weight</li>
<li>Turns recent performance into next-set targets</li>
<li>Keeps weekly muscle targets and recovery context in view</li>
<li>Makes deload guidance visible when the current evidence supports it</li>
</ol>
<p>That is the difference between a passive logbook and a training system that helps you act on your history. The useful test is not whether an app promises faster gains; it is whether the record makes a sensible next decision easier to repeat.</p>

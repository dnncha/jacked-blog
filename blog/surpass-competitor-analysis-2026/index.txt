<h2 id="the-short-version">The short version</h2>
<p>Surpass does not need to beat one app at everything. It needs to make one
valuable promise unusually credible:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Log a productive set quickly, understand the next decision, and keep a
trustworthy record of why the plan changed.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The relevant market is not one neat category. It has at least four jobs:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Fast logging:</strong> record sets with as little interruption as possible.</li>
<li><strong>Structured strength:</strong> follow a known progression model with few decisions.</li>
<li><strong>Hypertrophy coaching:</strong> manage volume, effort, exercise choice, and fatigue.</li>
<li><strong>Flexible programming:</strong> let an advanced lifter define the rules themselves.</li>
</ol>
<p>The best commercial opportunity for Surpass is the overlap between the first
and third jobs: a native logger that is fast enough for the gym floor and
intelligent enough to explain the next target. That is a strategic hypothesis,
not a measured market fact.</p>
<h2 id="evidence-rules">Evidence rules</h2>
<p>This map separates three kinds of statements:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Confirmed:</strong> visible in an official App Store listing or official product
page on the date above.</li>
<li><strong>Vendor-stated:</strong> a claim made by the competitor, such as a user count. It
is recorded as a claim, not treated as independent market measurement.</li>
<li><strong>Hypothesis:</strong> a product opportunity or weakness to test. It is not a fact
about another company's users, revenue, retention, or internal systems.</li>
</ul>
<p>App Store ratings are a public demand and discoverability signal. They are not
revenue, downloads, retention, satisfaction of every user, or proof of better
training outcomes. This map contains no invented competitor revenue or cohort
metrics.</p>

<h2 id="what-the-map-actually-tells-us">What the map actually tells us</h2>
<h3 id="the-price-ladder-is-real-the-outcome-ladder-is-unproven">The price ladder is real; the outcome ladder is unproven</h3>
<p>The public listings show a broad price range: low-cost logging and
scriptability, mid-priced smart planning, and premium hypertrophy coaching.
That does not tell us which product has the best retention or lifetime value.
Surpass should not choose a price by copying a competitor. It should first
prove that users experience a recurring value event: a logged set leads to a
credible next target, and the next session makes the value of the record
obvious.</p>
<h3 id="programming-claims-are-easy-to-copy-decision-trust-is-harder">Programming claims are easy to copy; decision trust is harder</h3>
<p>Nearly every major product now uses words such as “AI,” “personalized,” or
“progression.” The defensible surface is the chain of evidence:</p>
<ol>
<li>what the user logged;</li>
<li>what rule or model was applied;</li>
<li>what changed in the next target;</li>
<li>what the user can override; and</li>
<li>what is retained if the app is interrupted or offline.</li>
</ol>
<p>That chain should be visible without forcing a beginner to understand the full
training engine.</p>
<h3 id="video-breadth-is-a-competitor-strength-not-a-claim-surpass-should-fake">Video breadth is a competitor strength, not a claim Surpass should fake</h3>
<p>Alpha, Fitbod, Hevy, JEFIT, and RP publicly advertise exercise videos or
technique media. Surpass should not scrape or ship unlicensed media. Its
credible alternative is source-backed setup/form guidance, equipment-aware
selection, and a first-set cue whose limits are stated plainly.</p>
<h3 id="social-features-are-not-automatically-a-retention-strategy">Social features are not automatically a retention strategy</h3>
<p>Hevy and JEFIT publicly emphasize community features. That is confirmed product
positioning, not proof that social mechanics caused retention. Surpass should
only add accountability or sharing when an owner-controlled cohort shows that
it improves productive training behavior without increasing privacy cost or
workout friction.</p>
<h2 id="the-surpass-advantage-to-build-deeply">The Surpass advantage to build deeply</h2>
<p>The current product direction should concentrate on four linked advantages:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Fast first value:</strong> one credible starter plan, one obvious first set, and
no unnecessary configuration before useful training begins.</li>
<li><strong>Explainable next-set coaching:</strong> load, reps, effort, evidence, and reason
stay together; manual edits remain safe and visible.</li>
<li><strong>Weekly programming context:</strong> muscle dose, phase intent, recovery signals,
and the next review action are readable without false precision.</li>
<li><strong>Trustworthy local durability:</strong> sets remain safe through offline use,
interruption, retry, relaunch, and sync failure; unavailable reads are
labelled instead of becoming fabricated empty history.</li>
</ol>
<p>This is a product strategy hypothesis. It becomes a competitive advantage only
if released behavior and cohort evidence show better first-workout completion,
repeat training, and paid value than the relevant alternatives.</p>
<h2 id="opportunity-ranking">Opportunity ranking</h2>
<p>The following ranking uses the required expected-value model qualitatively:</p>
<p><strong>market demand × willingness to pay × retention potential × discoverability ×
competitor weakness × Surpass execution advantage.</strong></p>
<p>| Opportunity | Demand / willingness signal | Retention potential | Execution edge | Next proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explainable hypertrophy progression | High direct relevance; premium willingness is plausible but unmeasured | High hypothesis because the value can recur every set and session | Existing V2 recommendation, receipt, weekly dose, and recovery surfaces | Mature first-set and second-workout cohorts with edit/accept and return outcomes |
| Fast, durable workout logging | High: Strong, Hevy, and Fitbod ratings show strong public demand signals | High if users trust that no work is lost | Native local-first event and recovery work already exist | Offline/interruption/relaunch test on the exact release candidate plus time-to-first-set telemetry |
| Beginner-to-intermediate hypertrophy starter plan | High discoverability and broad addressable need; willingness unknown | Medium/high if the first plan reaches a productive second week | Recommended path and equipment-aware planning exist | First-workout completion, planned-versus-completed sets, D1/D7 return by onboarding path |
| Transparent migration and plan sharing | Demand is visible in competitor import/share positioning; size unknown | Medium; handoff must lead to a real first set | Source-bounded preview/import path exists | Link-open to copied-plan first-set cohort, with failure and cancellation rates |
| Social accountability | Confirmed competitor positioning, but causal value unknown | Unknown until tested | Lower current execution advantage than core coaching | Small consented experiment after core activation data is available |</p>
<h2 id="measurement-and-decision-rules">Measurement and decision rules</h2>
<p>Do not declare a competitor win from a feature checklist, a local screenshot,
App Store ratings, or a public crawl. The minimum operating readout is:</p>
<ul>
<li>onboarding completion by path;</li>
<li>first-workout ready;</li>
<li>first set logged;</li>
<li>first workout saved;</li>
<li>planned versus completed hard sets;</li>
<li>second workout within 1, 7, and 30 days, using mature denominators;</li>
<li>recommendation accepted, edited, or replaced;</li>
<li>trial start, trial-to-paid, churn, refund, and net revenue where authorized;</li>
<li>visible errors, persistence failures, and recovery retries.</li>
</ul>
<p>Missing exports must remain <code>null</code>, not zero. Every experiment needs a
predeclared success metric, guardrail, exposure marker, and rollback condition.</p>
<h2 id="what-surpass-should-not-do">What Surpass should not do</h2>
<ul>
<li>publish competitor revenue, funding, team size, or retention without a
primary, current source;</li>
<li>claim better hypertrophy or adherence from product capability alone;</li>
<li>copy protected code, media, screenshots, or branding;</li>
<li>hide the free experience so users cannot reach the core value;</li>
<li>use fake reviews, dark patterns, spam, or misleading “AI” promises;</li>
<li>turn a missing analytics connector into a claim about zero traffic.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>
<p>The strongest competitors already cover large parts of logging, programming,
media, social, and automation. Surpass wins by making the whole decision loop
feel calmer and more trustworthy: start quickly, log without interruption,
understand the next target, and keep the evidence intact. That is a narrower
promise than “the best fitness app,” but it is concrete enough to measure and
deep enough to become a durable product advantage.</p>

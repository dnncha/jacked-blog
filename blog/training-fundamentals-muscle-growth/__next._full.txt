<h2 id="the-three-mechanisms-of-hypertrophy">The Three Mechanisms of Hypertrophy</h2>
<p>Muscle grows through three pathways:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Mechanical tension</strong> — lifting heavy things</li>
<li><strong>Muscle damage</strong> — controlled degradation</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic stress</strong> — the burn</li>
</ol>
<p>All three matter. Your training should target all three.</p>
<h2 id="compound-vs-isolation-exercises">Compound vs Isolation Exercises</h2>
<h3 id="compound-movements">Compound Movements</h3>
<p>Multi-joint exercises working multiple muscle groups:</p>
<ul>
<li>Squat, deadlift, bench press</li>
<li>Pull-ups, rows, overhead press</li>
<li>Lunges, dips</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Why they matter:</strong> Maximum muscle activation, hormone response, strength foundation.</p>
<h3 id="isolation-exercises">Isolation Exercises</h3>
<p>Single-joint, single-muscle:</p>
<ul>
<li>Bicep curls, tricep extensions</li>
<li>Leg extensions, hamstring curls</li>
<li>Lateral raises</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Why they matter:</strong> Target lagging muscles, volume accumulation.</p>
<h3 id="the-verdict">The Verdict</h3>
<p>Prioritize compounds. Add isolation for volume and specifics.</p>
<p><strong>Suggested split:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>70% compound movements</li>
<li>30% isolation work</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="rep-ranges-the-myth-vs-reality">Rep Ranges: The Myth vs Reality</h2>
<p>Here's what the research actually shows:</p>
<p>| Rep Range | Best For |
|-----------|----------|
| 1-5 reps | Strength (neurological adaptation) |
| 6-12 reps | Hypertrophy (optimal zone) |
| 12-20 reps | Endurance/muscular endurance |
| 20+ reps | Metabolic stress (and cardio) |</p>
<p><strong>Truth:</strong> Total volume matters more than exact rep range. 5×10 at 60% builds muscle just as well as 5×5 at 80%.</p>
<h2 id="volume-how-much-is-enough">Volume: How Much is Enough?</h2>
<p><strong>Minimum effective dose:</strong> ~10 sets per muscle group per week</p>
<p><strong>Optimal range:</strong> 10-20 sets per muscle group</p>
<p><strong>Beyond 20:</strong> Diminishing returns, higher injury risk</p>
<h3 id="weekly-example-push-day">Weekly Example (Push Day)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Bench press: 4×8</li>
<li>Overhead press: 3×10</li>
<li>Incline dumbbell press: 3×10</li>
<li>Lateral raises: 3×15</li>
<li>Tricep pushdowns: 3×12</li>
</ul>
<p>= ~16 sets for chest, shoulders, triceps</p>
<h2 id="frequency-how-often">Frequency: How Often?</h2>
<p><strong>Minimum:</strong> 2x per muscle per week
<strong>Optimal:</strong> 2-3x per muscle per week
<strong>Maximum:</strong> 4x (advanced, not necessary)</p>
<p>More frequency = more protein synthesis stimulation = more growth.</p>
<h2 id="rest-periods">Rest Periods</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Compound lifts:</strong> 2-3 minutes (strength/power)</li>
<li><strong>Isolation lifts:</strong> 1-2 minutes (hypertrophy)</li>
<li><strong>Don't rest too long:</strong> Keeps metabolic stress high</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The Bottom Line</h2>
<ol>
<li>Prioritize compound movements</li>
<li>Hit 10-20 sets per muscle per week</li>
<li>Train each muscle 2-3x weekly</li>
<li>Progressive overload is non-negotiable</li>
<li>Recovery enables growth — sleep 8 hours</li>
</ol>

# Autoprogression: How to Choose the Next Training Load

Autoprogression means allowing the completed work to influence the next prescription. Instead of adding a fixed amount every week regardless of the day, you define a target and adjust when the observed reps, effort, or technique justify it.

This is a decision rule, not a guarantee of optimal adaptation. The same rule can be used with a notebook, spreadsheet, coach, or app.

## Why fixed progressions can miss the day

A fixed plan assumes that the training max, recovery, exercise setup, and effort are close enough to the assumptions used when it was written. Real sessions vary because of sleep, stress, illness, travel, recent volume, and equipment.

Autoprogression does not measure all of those causes. It simply gives the plan permission to respond to what happened in the set.

## What the evidence supports

A 2025 systematic review and network meta-analysis compared APRE, RPE-based training, velocity-based training, and percentage-based loading ([Huang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40791980/)). Autoregulated approaches ranked favorably in some maximal-strength analyses, but the paper does not establish that every autoregulated programme produces larger gains, nor that a software implementation is equivalent to the research protocols.

The practical inference is modest: an autoregulated rule is reasonable to test when day-to-day performance varies. It should be judged by whether it produces better decisions and sustainable training, not by a claim of guaranteed superiority.

## Three workable rules

### 1. Rep-range progression

Choose a range, such as 8–12 reps, and a load that lets you finish with the intended effort.

- If you reach the top of the range at the planned RIR with stable technique, add a small increment next time.
- If you remain inside the range but the effort is higher than planned, keep the load.
- If you miss the bottom of the range or technique changes, reduce the load or stop the set.

### 2. RIR or RPE progression

Set an effort target, such as 2 RIR. Record the estimated reps left at the end of the set. Increase only when the target reps and effort are repeatable across sessions.

RIR is a skill and can be inaccurate, especially for new lifters or unfamiliar exercises. That is a reason to use trends and conservative changes, not a reason to pretend the number is objective.

### 3. Performance-trend progression

Compare the same exercise, rep range, rest period, and technique across several sessions. A single strong or weak set is not enough to redefine the programme. Use the trend to decide whether to add load, maintain, reduce volume, or investigate sleep, pain, and recovery.

## What an app should do

Good autoprogression software should:

- show the target and the reason for a suggestion;
- retain the actual load, reps, and effort;
- allow substitutions and manual overrides;
- treat pain and illness as a stop or modification signal;
- avoid calling a recommendation a diagnosis or injury prediction.

Surpass can reduce the arithmetic and keep the progression history connected to the workout log. That is different from claiming that an algorithm knows your exact readiness.

## A small four-week test

1. Choose one or two stable exercises.
2. Pick a rep range and effort target.
3. Keep rest periods and technique reasonably consistent.
4. Apply the same adjustment rule after each session.
5. Record missed reps, pain, sleep disruption, and unusual stress.
6. Review the trend at the end of the block.

If the rule repeatedly pushes you into poor technique or pain, it is not working for that exercise—even if the spreadsheet says the next increment is due.

## The bottom line

Autoprogression is a practical way to connect the next load to the work you actually completed. Research on autoregulation supports it as a reasonable strategy, especially for maximal-strength programming, but does not prove a universal advantage or remove the need for judgment.

## Applying this article

Run the four-week test on one exercise before changing your whole programme. Keep the rule visible, write down why the load changed, and stop or modify the plan when symptoms or technique—not just the target number—say to.

## Limits of the evidence

Autoregulation studies differ in exercises, effort targets, supervision, and outcomes. A research finding about a training method is not independent validation of any particular app.
